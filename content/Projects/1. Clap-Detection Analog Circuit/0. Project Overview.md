### Project Context

This initiative serves as the capstone project for the **Introduction to Electric Circuits** course (Semester 25-1). This course provides foundational understanding of analog circuits, covering essential components such as resistors, inductors, capacitors, and operational amplifiers (op-amps). Unlike other final projects which typically commence late in the term, this project began at the beginning of the semester due to its extensive scope and technical requirements.

### Objective

Our team aimed to engineer a circuit that activates an LED specifically when a clap sound is detected. A critical design constraint was **selectivity**: the system was required to react exclusively to a clap sound, effectively ignoring ambient noise and other distinct sounds.

> [!TIP] Note on Design Complexity
> The solution would have been trivial if we omitted the requirement for sound filtering, simply constructing a circuit triggered by any sound exceeding a specific decibel threshold. However, to increase the technical rigor and challenge of the project, we prioritized high-precision detection. This goal drove our development efforts throughout the semester. 

### Procedure

The overall procedure of this project was done as follows:

1. [[1. Problem Definition|Problem Definition]]
   - Defined the acoustic properties of a "clap" and determined the specific characteristics required for detection.
2. [[2. Circuit Design|Circuit Design]]
   - Designed the circuit based on the identified characteristics and conducted simulations to verify theoretical functionality.
3. [[3. Breadboard Prototype|Breadboard Prototype]]
   - Implemented the circuit on the breadboard to validate the circuit in a real-world environment before moving on to PCB.
4. [[4. PCB Design & Soldering|PCB Design & Soldering]]
   - After verifying the circuit with breadboard, we designed the PCB, ordered fabrication, and soldered the components to complete the PCB.
5. [[5. Demonstration|Demonstration]]
   - Final demonstration of our PCB to peers & professors.
